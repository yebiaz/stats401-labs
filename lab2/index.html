Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

This visualization explores the relationship among study hours, exam score, major, and year level.

Multivariate Scatterplot: Cities

At first I started out mimicking the logic of the practice students visualization. I made the numerical attributes be the axes, with temperature being x and population being y because it might indicate a correlation in how population changes as temp does. Development level, since ordinal, I mapped to a size, to indicate if there's any trend in size following population and temp as well. Regions were categorical and so I just made into color to let them stand out and be easily seen as families or clusters. The city names are visible when hovered (like the student names)

Non-scatterplot Compass Visualization of Cities

I also had a second idea to make the regions be visually represented by literally putting the regions spots where they might be on a compass. I believed this could be interesting. But I decided to bring the regions together into their own clusters, so I took the average of the populations and temps. This means that some of the individual data points are lost, we don't know if the average is actually representative, especially for West and North that you can see are not so clustered in the scatterplot. Population size is again instinctively the size of the bubbles and the temperatures are more instinctively red for hot and blue for cold. Development level I decided a thick outline being high development. Even though this visualization isn't the best, you can still tell higher temperatures bring higher population and higher development. East and West regions seem to be nice spots to live in. (*Disclosure: I did have have to learn and understand finding the average of the values. Claude also helped me with the more complex and extensive legends I wanted to add to help with understanding.)

Compass Visualization of Cities - no averages

Last version I wanted to play with! I figured an improvement could be having individual bubbles for each city in their region cluster. Interestingly, you can see how the previous visualization with the averages might be representative for some regions but misleading for others.
