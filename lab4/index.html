Lab 4:Cleaning Web Data for Visualization / Sentiment Analysis

Dataset:Trump Tweets
Source:Kaggle: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/austinreese/trump-tweets?resource=download

Sentiment counts by year

Average sentiment score by year

I used a Kaggle dataset of Trump's tweets over the years. I filtered for tweets written by Trump himself, removed duplicates, parsed the dates (since I wanted to see trends over years), did category standardization and text normalization as taught. Many columns were completely or partially empty so I dropped the geo, mentions, and hashtags columns. ID and link/urls I found no analytic use for, so I dropped. I found that the numeric fields had no weird numbers/formats or missing values. I also took 300 tweets from each year (instead of using all 40000 tweets which would have been an enormous runtime; though curious for future if I'd like to try again). 2009 and 2020 were partial years with only 56 and 181 tweets respectively so the visualization may be a bit off. I estimated sentiment as in the lab tasks with RoBERTa and got probabilities for negative, neutral, and positive classes and labelled tweets according to its highest class and calculated its continuous score. I encoded year (interval type) onto the x axis and tweet count (ratio) on the y-axis. I also encoded the ordinal sentiment classes by color. I also just wanted to make a second little visualization to see an average overall trend over the years so I made a line chart. It was interesting how in the first visualization, the negative tweets are going down, he's trying to gather support, it's nearing the election, and then in 2018, positive and negative tweets crossover with positive decreasing and negative increasing as he goes through presidency. You can see a slightly similar matching trend in the second visualization. But there is a peak of negative comments in 2011 (also in the second visualization, there's a huge fall in negativity), I wonder if this is a true reflection of what he was doing in 2011 or a limitation of the dataset and code.
